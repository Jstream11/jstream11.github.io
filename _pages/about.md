I have been driven by an unwavering passion for health equity through science and technology, shaping my professional journey, where I focused on biochemistry, mathematics, and computer science.

I acquired invaluable research skills at Weill Cornell Medical School, where I had the privilege of working alongside seasoned researchers in two distinct laboratories. The Scandura Lab investigated hematopoietic malignancies due to myeloproliferative disorders and I contributed to mouse colony management and DNA cloning operations, exposing myself to genotyping, primer design, and DNA sequencing. After a year, I transitioned to the Multiparametric In Situ Imaging Laboratory, where I explored the realms of digital pathology, image analysis, and spatial transcriptomics. Working closely with various investigators, I optimized multiplex immunofluorescent antibody panels for tissue staining, leveraging software packages including Akoya-InForm and Indica Labs-HALO for digital image analysis. Additionally, I played a key role in optimizing spatial transcriptomic protocols for the GeoMx platform. These endeavors culminated in the opportunity to present a poster at the United States and Canadian Academy of Pathology 2023 Annual Meeting in New Orleans, focusing on the megakaryocytic microenvironment.

These experiences helped me discover my passion for deciphering the intricate complexities of data and its meaningful application, spurring me to pursue further education and training in bioinformatics at Boston University.

The past few years, I have been a bioinformatician in the Computer Core at the Channing Division of Network Medicine at Brigham and Women's Hospital. Here, I build, maintain, and run bioinformatics pipelines for multi-omic data validation, enabling reproducible, scalable analysis across projects. I work with Snakemake and Nextflow workflow managers and have experience in transcriptomics (bulk rnaseq and mirna) and epigenetics. Currently, I am upgrading an inhouse DNA methylation pipeline (utilizing minfi and meffil R packages for methylation data) to support the Illumina EPIC v2 array and developing methods to harmonize data across array versions while maintaining data integrity. I also manage sample quality control for large-cohort studies(LTRC, VDAART, COPDGene). I lead the quality control of over 10,000 samples of DNA methylation data for the RECOVER Long COVID study, collaborating with systems biology experts to deliver analysis-ready datasets. I have also developed and deployed RShiny dashboards for sample tracking across more than 40,000 samples in multiple omics, giving teams real-time visibility into project status.

I enjoy working in a collaborative, data driven environment, where I can share my expertise while learning from those around me.

In my free time, I play softball, reliving my glory days playing at NYU, and knit. I love to meet up with other knitters around boston!
